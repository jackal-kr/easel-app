Easel Help

Easel is a simple paint program for quick pictures and touch-ups, in the manner of Paint in Windows XP.

Pan mode

Pan mode is the tool Easel starts with. Dragging moves the view and never changes the picture, so you can open a picture, look around and zoom before you edit it. The secondary button opens a menu with Paste, Paste From…, Select All, Zoom In, Zoom Out, Normal Size and Clear Image. A paste that lands switches to Select.

Tools

The toolbox's tools, in its order. The primary button draws in the foreground color, the secondary button in the background color. The other button cancels a drag. Esc cancels the drag of a shape, a selection or a text box, but not a stroke of Pencil, Brush, Airbrush or Eraser. The options of the chosen tool are under the toolbox. Line, Curve, the shapes and Brush draw with Sharp edges, in whole pixels, or with Smooth edges, whose edge pixels blend into the picture: choose under their options; Preferences sets which they start with.

Pan

Moves the view. No options.

Magnifier

A press with either button sets the zoom to the chosen level, or back to 100 % if it is already there. Options: the zoom levels.

Select

Marks a rectangle. Options: Opaque, or Transparent, which leaves the background color out.

Free-Form Select

Marks the shape you trace. Options as Select.

Eraser

Paints the background color. Options: its size.

Pick Color

Takes the color of the pixel you press with the primary button into the foreground color and with the secondary button into the background color, then goes back to the tool before it.

Fill

Fills the area of one color you press. No options.

Gradient

Fades from the color to transparent, so that a second gradient lies over the first. A drag sets its direction and length, Enter puts it down, Esc drops it.

Pencil

Draws lines one pixel wide. No options.

Brush

Paints with a tip. Options: the tip's shape and size.

Airbrush

Sprays while the button is held. Options: the spray's size.

Text

A drag opens a text box. See Text below.

Line

A straight line. Shift keeps it to steps of 45°. Options: its width.

Curve

A line, then two presses to bend it. Options: its width. Enter puts it down, Esc drops it.

Rectangle

Shift makes a square. Options: the width, and outline, outline and fill, or fill.

Rounded Rectangle

As Rectangle, with round corners.

Ellipse

Shift makes a circle. Options as Rectangle.

Polygon

Each press adds a side, and a double-click closes it. Options as Rectangle. Enter puts it down, Esc drops it.

Selections

A marked part can be moved by a drag or the arrow keys, copied by a drag with Ctrl, and stamped by a drag with Shift. Enter or Esc puts it down, Delete clears it. Cut, Copy and Paste act on it.

Text

A box holds styled text. Its font card chooses the family, the size and the styles, and a swatch colors the selected text. Esc puts the box down, and a press outside it does too. Until then the text can be changed.

Image menu

Flip, Rotate, Stretch and Skew…, Invert Colors, Attributes… for the picture's size, and Clear Image. With a selection, flip, rotate, stretch, skew and invert act on it.

Remove Background makes the background of the picture, or of the selection, transparent. It finds the background by its colors and edges: it removes a background that differs in color from the subject, and it does not recognise subjects. Its Tolerance starts at a value read from the border, and the picture shows the result as you change it. A pale subject on a pale background, and soft edges, need a lower Tolerance; a drawing of lines on a smooth background may be filled in. To fix the result by hand, click a piece of the preview to keep or remove it, or turn on Keep this or Remove this and paint over the parts; Ctrl+Z takes back the last click or stroke.

View menu

The zoom items, Show Grid, Grid…, the panels, and Show Thumbnail, a small card that shows the picture at 100 % while you work zoomed in.

Preferences

Preferences holds the look of the window, the language and the panels, the defaults for drawing and for new pictures, and how files are saved. A change the window can show shows at once, and the others wait. OK or Apply keeps them, and Cancel takes them back.

Saving

Save As and Copy To have a file type list. By extension lets the end of the name decide: .png, .jpg, .bmp, .tif or .webp, and a name with none is a PNG. Choosing a format decides the format, and the end of the name changes to match it. The list also chooses which files the dialog shows, so it can hide a file; typing that file's name still replaces it, after a question.

Printing

Print… sends the whole picture to a printer or a file, after putting down anything that waits. The print dialog chooses the paper, the orientation, the scale, the pages and the copies, and its Preview shows the pages. Its tab Picture has the rest. Fit on one page shrinks a large picture to one sheet and never enlarges it; turned off, it prints at 96 pixels to the inch, and the tab says how many sheets that takes. Pixels says how the picture's pixels reach the paper: Sharp keeps each a hard square, Smooth when shrunk averages them where a printer's dot covers several, and Smooth blends between them.

Undo

Undo takes back one step and Redo brings it back. While a text box is open, they act on the box's edits.

Using only the keyboard

Tab and Shift+Tab go from one panel to the next: the header bar, the toolbox, the tool's options, the canvas and the palette. F6 goes by whole panels. In a panel the arrows move the focus and Space chooses.

On the canvas a black and white square, the marker, shows a pixel. The arrows move it, and Space presses there as a mouse button does; move it and press Space again to release. Shift+Space uses the secondary button. Fill and the Magnifier act at the one press, and Esc cancels a press with any tool. With a selection, the arrows move it and the marker goes along; Alt with an arrow moves the marker alone. F7 says the marker's place and color to a screen reader, and the Menu key opens the menu there.

Handles have commands instead: Stretch and Skew… for a selection, Attributes… for the picture. With a text box open, Alt with an arrow moves it, and Alt+Shift with an arrow moves its far corner. A pointer outside the picture has no keyboard way.

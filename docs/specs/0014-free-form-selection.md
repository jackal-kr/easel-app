# 0014 Free-Form Selection

Status: implemented
Depends on: 0001, 0002, 0004, 0005, 0006, 0010, 0013

## Why
XP Paint's Free-Form Select marks any shape drawn with the pointer, not only a rectangle, so that a part of the picture with an
irregular edge can be moved, copied or cleared. XP also lets a selection leave a trail while it is dragged (Shift, "smear") and
lets it be stamped where it is (Shift-click). This feature brings the Free-Form Select tool, which moves, copies, clears and
puts down like the rectangle of 0013, and the smear and the stamp for both selection tools.
Decisions by JOD on 2026-10-20: stretching a selection by its eight handles is not here but the feature selection-handles after
this one (JOD's mock-up draws the handles round the bounding rectangle of the shape; here a free-form selection has none); the
smear leaves a copy at every pixel step, with no gaps; the outline of both selection tools gets a thicker line at a high zoom, a translucent blue tint over the selected pixels and dashes that move (after JOD saw mock-ups); a Shift-click stamps only on the selection; a free-form selection is
shown by an outline along the edge of its pixels and no bounding rectangle (at M2 of the build it was for a while the traced line
itself, chords across the pixels; JOD chose the pixel edges again on 2026-10-21, after seeing it at 800 % with the grid). The mock-up of 2026-10-20 (the Free-Form Select button in the toolbox
after Select, the same two mode buttons below the toolbox, the shape outlined by a dashed line) is described in REQ-0014-01 and -05.

## Requirements
Throughout, **the shape** of a free-form selection is its set of picture pixels, and its **bounding rectangle** is the smallest
rectangle that holds them. Everything REQ-0013 says of a selection holds for a free-form one (marked and floating, the lift, the
hole, the stamps and the put-down as one undo step, Esc, Enter, Undo, the arrow keys, Clear Selection, Select All, the context
menu, drags that do not finish, memory, blocked input) except where this spec says otherwise; where REQ-0013 says "its
rectangle" for the pixels, a free-form selection means its shape: only the pixels of the shape are lifted, leave a hole, are
moved, copied and put down, and a press is on the selection only on a pixel of the shape.

### The tool
- REQ-0014-01 The toolbox shall show Free-Form Select, with the tooltip "Free-Form Select", right after Select in the final order of REQ-0006-01, so that the toolbox is, in two columns, row by row: Pan, Magnifier / Select, Free-Form Select / Eraser, Pick Color / Fill, Gradient / Pencil, Brush / Airbrush, Line / Curve, Rectangle / Rounded Rectangle, Ellipse / Polygon. Its icon is a dashed lasso-like outline as in JOD's mock-up. Clicking it makes it the active tool (REQ-0001-09); clicking it while it is active keeps the selection as it is. Numpad + and Numpad − do nothing with it, and Ctrl+Numpad + and − keep zooming.
- REQ-0014-02 While Free-Form Select is active, the options panel shall show the two mode buttons of Select, Opaque (chosen at each start) and Transparent, drawn and worded as REQ-0013-02 says (as the Eraser's, not the mock-up's dots: JOD's choice at M1 of 0013). The mode is one choice shared with Select: choosing it with either tool is the mode of both, and it is not a setting.
- REQ-0014-03 The status bar hint (REQ-0004-09) shall be "Selects a free-form part of the picture to move, copy, or edit." While Free-Form Select is active the cursor over the canvas area shall be the crosshair of REQ-0010-15, and over the selection (a pixel of its shape) the move cursor; the resize cursor wins over a canvas handle (REQ-0004-17), also where the handle's hit area covers a pixel of the selection, and a press there is a resize, not a press on the selection. While a path is traced the cursor stays the crosshair, and during a move, smear or stamp press the move cursor. (Pointer note, Selection handles, 2026-10-22: over a handle of the selection the cursor is its resize cursor.)

### Marking a free-form selection
- REQ-0014-04 When the user presses the primary button with Free-Form Select in the canvas area, not on the selection and not on a canvas resize handle, the app shall first put down a floating selection and remove any selection (REQ-0013-11), then trace a path while the button is held, without changing the picture: the path is a line along the grid lines of the picture (the edges between pixels), through the corners of the grid nearest to the successive pointer positions, starting at the corner nearest to the press. Between two corners it follows the 8-connected line that the Pencil draws between them (REQ-0001-20, taken on the corners), each diagonal step made of a horizontal grid segment and then a vertical one. A press may start outside the picture but inside the canvas area (not in a handle's hit area, which lies partly inside the picture), and while the button is held the pointer may go outside the picture, the canvas area or the window (REQ-0010-21); the path is traced there too. If putting a floating selection down by this press fails (REQ-0013-19), the dialog shows and no path starts. The release closes the path the same way, from the last corner to the first. The shape is then the pixels that the closed path encloses, limited to the picture, and the selection is marked. A pixel is enclosed when no walk from it to a neighbouring pixel, never crossing a grid segment of the path, reaches outside the path's extent. A press and release with no movement to another corner (a click), a path that encloses no pixel (a straight drag, an out and back along the same line), and a path whose shape has no pixel inside the picture, make no selection. Shift and Ctrl change nothing of the tracing. (Pointer note, Selection handles, 2026-10-22: "resize handle" here means the picture's; a primary press on a selection handle stretches the selection and neither puts it down nor removes it.)
- REQ-0014-05 The selection shall be outlined, marked or floating, by its path (JOD, 2026-10-21, from his reference of the Easel at 800 % with the grid): one line along the grid segments of the closed path, its thickness as REQ-0014-17 says, drawn as the grid lines are (on the device pixel that starts at the grid line, so never across the inside of a pixel, at any zoom), in dashes alternating black and white, black first, their length and their movement as REQ-0014-17 says, counted along the path from its first corner as one continuous line, the closing segment last, over the picture and the canvas area (also where the path went outside the picture) and not part of the picture. While the path is traced the same line is shown without the closing segment; the release adds the closing segment and nothing else moves, no band and no second line. A path that crosses itself keeps its inner loops in the line. The line scrolls with the picture, moves with the selection with the same dashes, keeps its look as REQ-0014-17 says at every zoom, and its dashes are counted again at a new zoom. No bounding rectangle and no handle is drawn here: the eight handles round the bounding rectangle are the feature selection-handles. (Pointer note, Selection handles, 2026-10-22: the eight handles are now drawn round the bounding rectangle.)
- REQ-0014-06 The shape of a path that crosses itself shall be every pixel enclosed by any part of the path, the union of its loops (a bow tie is one shape of both lobes; JOD, 2026-10-20). A path that encloses no pixel, a straight drag or an out and back along the same line, shall make no selection, as a click does (JOD, 2026-10-21, replacing the decision of 2026-10-20 that such a path selects the pixels of its line).
- REQ-0014-07 The status bar shall show the selection's size as "W × H" (REQ-0013-06) for the bounding rectangle of its shape: while the path is traced, that of the box of its corners so far limited to the picture, in pixels (nothing while the box has no pixel inside the picture); while a selection exists, its whole size (also when it floats partly outside the picture); nothing otherwise. The pixels of the bounding rectangle outside the shape are not part of the selection: they are not lifted, not part of the hole, not written at the put-down, and a press on them is a press outside the selection.

### Moving, clearing and the modes with a shape
- REQ-0014-08 Moving a free-form selection (REQ-0013-07 to REQ-0013-10, with Ctrl as REQ-0013-09) shall lift only the pixels of the shape. The hole it leaves (REQ-0013-08) is the shape's place: those pixels become fully transparent (#00000000) in Transparent mode and the background color current at the lift in Opaque mode; the rest of the bounding rectangle is not touched. The floating pixels keep their colors in both modes (REQ-0013-13), and the put-down writes only them, each replacing the picture's pixel in all four channels (REQ-0013-11).
- REQ-0014-09 Clear Selection (REQ-0013-14) shall act on the shape: on a marked free-form selection its pixels become fully transparent in Transparent mode and the background color in Opaque mode, as one undo step (none if no pixel changes); on a floating one its pixels are discarded and the hole and the copies stay. Select All (REQ-0013-15) shall mark the whole picture as a rectangle selection with Select, whatever the selection was.
- REQ-0014-10 Choosing Select while a free-form selection exists, or Free-Form Select while a rectangle exists, shall put the selection down or remove it (REQ-0013-11, REQ-0013-12), as any other tool change does: a selection belongs to no tool but is not kept across tools.

### Smear and stamp (Select and Free-Form Select)
- REQ-0014-11 While Shift is held at the press of a move drag on the selection (without Ctrl; Ctrl+Shift acts as Ctrl), the app shall smear: the selection moves as REQ-0013-07 says, no hole is left (as with Ctrl, REQ-0013-09), and a copy is left at every place the selection passes, so that a drag leaves a continuous trail: between each two successive pointer positions (picture pixels), a copy stays at each whole-pixel place on the 8-connected line (REQ-0001-20) between the selection's two places, the place it came from included and the place it moves to excluded. A copy is the selection's pixels as they would be put down there (REQ-0013-11), laid in order, the later one over the earlier, the floating selection over all. A marked selection is lifted at the first movement to another pixel, as REQ-0013-07 says. Shift pressed or released after the press changes nothing; the arrow keys do not smear. The copies belong to the one undo step of REQ-0013-11.
- REQ-0014-12 When the user presses the primary button with Shift on a floating selection (on a pixel of it) and releases it without moving to another pixel, the app shall stamp: a copy of the selection's pixels is left where it is, as they would be put down there, and the selection keeps floating where it is. The stamp is made at the release; if the release never comes, the selection ends as last shown (REQ-0014-14) and no stamp is made. With Ctrl and Shift the click is Ctrl's (REQ-0013-09: no stamp). On a marked selection a Shift-click changes nothing, and so does a Shift press with a movement that never reaches another pixel; a movement to another pixel lifts it and smears. A Shift-click that is not on the selection is a press of the tool (REQ-0014-04 or REQ-0013-04, which Shift changes only as REQ-0013-04 says). The stamp belongs to the one undo step of REQ-0013-11; if no pixel differs at the end, no step is added.
- REQ-0014-13 If the memory for a copy of a smear or a stamp cannot be had, then the app shall show the dialog of REQ-0013-19 ("Could Not Move the Selection", "There is not enough memory to move this selection."), end the drag there, and put the selection back where and as it was before this drag (marked, if this drag lifted it, with the lift undone as REQ-0013-18 says; floating, if it floated), with this drag's copies gone; the release then does nothing. The memory to lift the shape (its pixels and its mask) is that of REQ-0013-19, with the same dialog.

### Drags that do not finish, memory, blocked input
- REQ-0014-14 During the tracing of a path, the app shall cancel it when the other button is pressed, Esc is pressed or Undo is chosen (after Undo, REQ-0002-09 applies until the release): no selection is made, a put-down made by the press stays, and any release after that does nothing. Enter, Delete, Select All, Redo and the arrow keys do nothing during it. If the release never arrives (REQ-0007-18) or the window loses focus (REQ-0010-19), the path is closed and made as last shown, as if released there; when a press shows that the release was lost, that press then acts as a press of its own. Save or Quit requested while the button is held makes the selection as last shown first (marked, so Save keeps it), then goes on. If the release never arrives and no pointer event follows, the path is the pixels traced so far, one pixel if the pointer never moved, which makes no selection. "As last shown" means as a release at the last pointer position would make it. A smear or a stamp is cancelled, ended as last shown and put down by exactly the events of REQ-0013-18: the other button, Esc or Undo put the selection back where it was before this drag, this drag's copies gone, and it stays selected; a lost release or focus ends the drag as last shown; Save, Save As, New, Open and Quit put it down first. (Pointer note, Clipboard, 2026-10-22: Cut, Copy, Paste, Copy To… and Paste From… are ignored during a trace too, REQ-0016-02.)
- REQ-0014-15 If the memory for the path or the shape cannot be had, then at the release the app shall show an error dialog with the heading "Could Not Make the Selection" and the body "There is not enough memory to make this selection.", make no selection and change nothing beyond the put-down made by the press. The memory may run out while the path is traced: the trace goes on as far as it can be kept, the path's last shown form stays, and the dialog shows at the release. A press with Free-Form Select shall be ignored when a press of Select is (REQ-0013-20), and with a dialog, a prompt or the color chooser open the keys act on that.
- REQ-0014-16 Tracing a long path, and smearing a selection along a long drag, shall keep the pointer's response within a frame (16 ms median, as the pointer-to-pixels budget of `docs/engineering.md` on a 1920 × 1080 view) up to a path of 100000 pointer positions on an 8000 × 8000 picture and to a smear of 2000 copies of a 400 × 300 selection and of 30000 copies of a 150 × 110 selection on a 2560 × 1600 picture, at 100 % and at the fit zoom, and the redraw of the outline of a long path shall not grow beyond that frame. `perf-0014` measures them and reports the numbers, which are recorded in the Plan; a case over budget goes to JOD before any fix, as in 0013, where the fit zoom of an 8000 × 8000 picture was accepted as over budget.
- REQ-0014-17 The outline of a selection shall be easy to see on any picture, with the grid on or off, for Select and Free-Form Select alike, marked or floating (JOD, 2026-10-21, after his mock-ups): (a) its thickness is 1 logical pixel while a picture pixel is less than 6 device pixels wide (at scale 1, up to 400 %) and 2 logical pixels from 6 (600 % and 800 %; JOD, 2026-10-21: thinner than the first try of 3 at 800 %, to be judged by eye), and each of its dashes and gaps is 4 times its thickness long, in logical pixels; (b) the pixels of the selection, those of the rectangle or of the shape, are covered by a translucent tint of the accent color that the user chose in the GNOME settings (JOD, 2026-10-21; blue, #3584E4, where the system has none) at 12 % (JOD, 2026-10-28: lighter, so that what is inside shows; it was 28 %; the feature Preferences makes it a setting of its Appearance), over the picture and the grid and under the line, and the tint follows a change of the accent at once; it is not part of the picture, is not drawn for a path while it is traced, and moves with a floating selection; (c) once the button is released, the dashes of a marked or floating selection's line move along the line at 12 logical pixels a second, in the direction the line is counted (for a rectangle clockwise from its top-left corner, the four sides one continuous line), for as long as the selection is shown (JOD, 2026-10-21: they start when the left button is released); while a path is traced or a rectangle is dragged out they stand still, and they stand still too, with the first dash at the start of the line, when the user's settings turn animations off. The rectangle's line lies just outside its pixels, as REQ-0013-05 says, with the thickness, the dashes and the tint of this requirement.

## Edge cases
- A path that starts and ends at the same corner with movement between (a loop): the closing is nothing; the shape is the pixels the loop encloses, none if it encloses none.
- A path outside the picture that encloses a part of it: the shape is the enclosed pixels inside the picture only; one that surrounds the whole picture makes the whole picture the shape, as a rectangle would.
- A one-pixel shape (a path round one pixel, or clipping): it is marked, moved and cleared as any other.
- A selection floating wholly outside the picture and put down: nothing is written, the hole remains (one step; no step after a Ctrl lift).
- The shape moved back exactly onto its hole: no pixel differs, no step is added, in both modes.
- Shift pressed during tracing, a move or the arrow keys: nothing (REQ-0014-04, REQ-0014-11); Ctrl+Shift at the press of a move acts as Ctrl.
- A Shift press on a selection then a movement of less than a pixel (zoomed in): a click, a stamp; the first movement to another pixel makes it a smear that began with the copy at the place of the press, so a stamp then a smear leave the same first copy.
- A very long smear (a 10000 × 10000 selection dragged across the picture, or copies wholly outside the picture): each copy costs its place only (8 bytes) while it floats; the put-down step costs what REQ-0002-16 allows for the pixels it changes, and a step or a copy that cannot be had shows the dialog of REQ-0014-13.
- The same copy stamped twice at one place: the same pixels; no extra step cost beyond the pixels that differ.
- The same action again while its dialog is open: as 0011.
- For a state that waits: the mouse alone finishes a floating selection by a press outside it or another tool; color changes leave it as it is (REQ-0013-13); view changes keep it; tool changes put it down (REQ-0013-11, REQ-0014-10).

## Out of scope
- The eight handles of a selection and stretching it by them (the feature selection-handles), including the handles JOD's mock-up draws round the bounding rectangle of a free-form selection.
- Cut, Copy, Paste, Copy To…, Paste From…, the canvas menu outside a selection (Clipboard); Flip, Rotate, Stretch, Skew, Invert Colors on a selection (Image Transforms).
- A free-form selection that is not a single closed path: adding to or subtracting from a selection (Phase 2 at most).

## Plan
### Approach
A selection gets an optional **mask**: one byte per pixel of its bounding rectangle, 1 for a pixel of the shape (no mask = the
whole rectangle, as in 0013). Everything 0013 built takes the mask: the lift copies the mask into the float with the pixels,
the lay and the put-down skip pixels outside it, the hole is only the mask's pixels, a stamp is the masked float at a place,
Clear fills the masked pixels, and the hit test reads it. So the free-form selection reuses the one lay function, the one
put-down (`finish_selection`), the one cancel of a drag (`cancel_selection_drag`) and the one cancel of a float
(`cancel_float`) of 0013 unchanged in structure.
The path is a closed line along the grid lines, kept as the corners the pointer reached (8 bytes each). A pure core walker gives, between two
corners, the Pencil's 8-connected line taken on the corners with each diagonal step as a horizontal grid segment and a vertical one. The
shape is made at the release by a pure core function: the grid segments of the closed path are collected (each once), the picture's rows are cut by
the vertical segments into runs of pixels, runs of adjacent rows are joined where a column of their overlap has no horizontal segment between them,
and the runs joined to the outside (the rows above and below the path's extent and the ends of every row) are left out; what is left is the
shape, limited to the picture. Memory and time grow with the path's length in grid segments, not with its extent. The same walker draws the line:
the canvas keeps the corners of the path (while tracing, and with the selection afterwards, moved by the selection's offset) and draws, along the
walked segments, a white strip and black dashes counted along the path from its first corner.
The smear is a core function: from the float's place to a new one it stamps every place on the 8-connected line, the start
included and the end excluded, then moves the float. The stamp of a Shift-click is the existing `easel_selection_stamp`.

### Where it is put down, removed or cancelled
The same functions as 0013: `finish_selection` puts down (now writes only masked pixels, through the lay), `cancel_float`
forgets a float, `cancel_selection_drag` cancels a drag: for a trace it drops the path; for a move it undoes a lift the drag made,
for a smear it cuts the copies back to the count at the press (`sel_stamps0`), for a Ctrl copy it takes the one copy away
(`sel_stamped_here` kept, as built), each through `cut_copies`, and puts the float back. A failed smear ends in `end_failed_smear`.
The trace ends in one function, `end_lasso`, from the release, a lost release, a lost focus, Save and Quit.

### What grows
- The path: 8 bytes per pointer position (doubling, freed at the end of the trace; never handed to the history, so no shrink).
- The shape: the mask, 1 byte per pixel of the bounding rectangle (at most the picture: 100 MB for 10000 × 10000), held while
  the selection exists; the intervals while it is made: about 16 bytes per path pixel and row, freed at once.
- The float: its pixels (4 bytes) and its mask copy (1 byte) per pixel of the bounding rectangle; a stamp is 8 bytes (its place)
  for a Ctrl copy, a Shift-click and each copy of a smear (2000 copies: 16 kB).
- The put-down step: as 0013 (the log shrunk to its length, held-versus-counted tested for the masked put-down and the masked clear).

### Constitution check
Layering holds (the path, the shape, the edges, the masked lay and the smear in the libc core); P4 holds (hard-edged masks, no
blending); P5 holds (plain lay and per-frame outline first, `perf-0014` before anything faster); one undo step per put-down.

### Risks
- **The line (REQ-0014-05, JOD's reference of 2026-10-21).** One line, the same while the path is traced and after the release: a
  white strip and black dashes of 4 logical pixels along the grid segments of the walked path, each strip on the device pixel that
  starts at the grid line (as the grid is drawn, REQ-0004-12), so it lies on grid lines at every zoom (AS-22); the dashes are counted
  along the path from its first corner (the lattice dashes, in the core) and the canvas turns the picture pixels of a dash into
  logical pixels (4 / the zoom's logical pixels per picture pixel). The selection keeps the corners and draws the line moved by its
  offset. Core tests: the walker's steps, the dashes of a short closed path by hand, and a moved path's dashes equal the original's
  moved. (History: three earlier designs, the pixel border of the shape in T003 and T018, and a line through the centers of the
  path's pixels in T006, were built and removed; learning log.)
- **Moving dashes (REQ-0014-17 c)** need the canvas drawn again every frame while a selection or a traced path is shown: a tick callback on
  the frame clock that asks for a redraw and ends itself when none is shown or animations are off (the setting `gtk-enable-animations`);
  the dash phase (how far the pattern has advanced along the line) is the time times 12 logical pixels, taken modulo a dash and a gap and, for the free-form line, as picture pixels for the core's `easel_lattice_dashes`; the ticks run only while a marked or floating selection is shown and the button is up. Tests
  turn animations off (the phase is then 0) and one test turns them on. The cost of a canvas redraw a frame (tiles, grid, line, tint) is
  measured in T015 (fallback: redraw only the part of the canvas the line covers).
- **The cost of the line per frame** grows with the path's length in grid segments; a long path over 8000 × 8000 may hold hundreds of
  thousands. Measured in T015 with the long path of AS-14; the fallback, only after the measurement, is to merge the white
  strips of a straight run and to keep the part of the line out of view out of the snapshot.
- **The Pencil's line**: between two corners the walker takes the Pencil's 8-connected line (REQ-0001-20) on the corners; the lattice test
  compares the corners it visits with the pixels the Pencil stroke draws between the same two points (core).
- **A float's outside part** used the float's pixels directly; with a mask it must leave the outside-the-mask pixels
  transparent, checked by AS-13's rendering test in the task of the masked move.

### The line and the tint below 100 % (T015, JOD 2026-10-21)

(one rectangle per run of a picture row at 100 % and above). Identical at 100 % and above (`line_runs_draw_the_same`,

shape, so the number of pixels is not bounded); each goes red if the line or the tint moves by a whole picture pixel.

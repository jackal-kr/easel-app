# 0030 Brush options that read at a glance

Status: implemented
Reviewed: spec-reviewer, 2026-11-07
Depends on: 0001 (settings file), 0004 (status bar, Tool Box), 0006 (Brush, Airbrush, Eraser presets), 0008 (toolbox width), 0010 (line widths), 0011 (Curve, Polygon widths), 0020 (Preferences, Drawing rows, a failed write), 0021 (Frame time), 0022 (accessible names)

## Why
The Brush's twelve tips are small glyphs in two columns, and JOD could not tell their shapes and sizes apart (2026-11-05, Phase 2 item B). The same holds for the sizes of Airbrush, Eraser and the line width. This feature draws every size preset at its real size with its number, keeps the two columns, lets the user widen the toolbox to see the presets named in words, and shows the chosen preset in the status bar. Decided by JOD on 2026-11-07 from the mockups in `docs/reference/0030/brush-panel-mockup.png` (option C).

- REQ-0030-03 (Changes REQ-0006-07's look.) - REQ-0030-04 (Pointer note, Smooth shapes and brushes, 2026-11-10: the Sharp | Smooth well is drawn as the mode well, REQ-0039-01.) (Changes REQ-0006-10's look.) While the Eraser is active and the panel is in its well form, its four sizes shall stand in one row of one well, 4, 6, 8 and 10, each showing its square footprint as REQ-0030-02 does, no number; below it, apart, a second well holds the two modes side by side, Opaque then Transparent, drawn as today.

- REQ-0030-07 On a screen of scale 2, the footprints, discs and lines of REQ-0030-02 to REQ-0030-05 shall be drawn with two device pixels per picture pixel, each pixel a sharp square with only fully opaque and fully transparent device pixels; the wells, the labels and the cells scale as the rest of the window.

### The status bar
- REQ-0030-08 (Adds a part to REQ-0004-09; REQ-0021-12's place for Frame time is unchanged.) While Brush, Airbrush, Eraser, or a tool with the line widths is active, the status bar shall show the selected size preset in the words of REQ-0030-06 as its own part, directly to the smaller x of Frame time when it is shown, else of the pointer position's place (present also while the position is empty). The part's width is that of the active tool's longest name, so a change of preset does not move the hint. For the shapes it shows the width only, not the fill style. It changes at once when the preset changes, by a click or by Numpad + and − (REQ-0006-14, REQ-0010-04). For the other tools the part is not shown and takes no space. When the status bar is too narrow, the hint is cut first.

### A wider toolbox
- REQ-0030-09 (Changes REQ-0008-07: the toolbox keeps one width for every tool, and the user can now change it.) The edge between the toolbox column and the canvas area shall be dragged with the primary button to make the toolbox wider or narrower: the width changes by the pointer's horizontal movement since the press, from the default width of REQ-0008-07 up to a limit of 240 logical pixels times the text scale factor, and never more than half the window's width. The edge takes presses over a band 6 logical pixels wide; over it the pointer shows the desktop's left-right resize cursor; other buttons on it do nothing. The tool buttons keep their size and their two columns, centered in the column.
- REQ-0030-10 (Changed 2026-11-08, JOD: the named one-per-row form is dropped.) When the toolbox is wider than its default, the options panel shall keep its size and its form and stand centered under the tool buttons; the names of the presets stay in their tooltips and the status bar (REQ-0030-06, -08).
- REQ-0030-11 A double-click on the edge shall return the toolbox to its default width.
- REQ-0030-12 The width shall be kept in the settings file (REQ-0001-01) as `[view] toolbox-width`, a whole number: the width shown divided by the text scale factor, rounded to the nearest whole number; it is written when a drag that moved the edge ends, and after a double-click (then as the default width's value), and read at start-up. A press and release on the edge with no movement writes nothing. A value that is not a whole number or a missing key gives the default width; a value past a limit is taken as that limit. The file's syntax is REQ-0001-40's; a write that fails leaves the file as it is and shows nothing; the width applies for this run, a warning goes to the log, and the write is tried again at the end of the next drag (JOD, 2026-11-07). Preferences has no row for it, and its OK or Apply leaves the key as it is.
- REQ-0030-13 If the window loses focus or the button is released outside the window while the edge is dragged, then the drag shall end as REQ-0001-25 ends a stroke, with the width at the last pointer position, kept and written. Esc during the drag does nothing to it. The window shall not be made narrower than the toolbox's width and the canvas area's least width (JOD, 2026-11-08, R5); where the window has less than twice the kept width (a small screen, a large text size), the toolbox shows narrower (half the window, REQ-0030-09), never narrower than the default width, and the kept width does not change; the window growing again shows it again. The panel's form (REQ-0030-10) follows the width shown.
- REQ-0030-14 While the toolbox is hidden (Tool Box off, REQ-0004-27), the edge is hidden and takes no presses; shown again, the toolbox has the width it had.

### What does not change
- REQ-0030-15 The presets, their footprints, their defaults, Numpad + and −, what a preset paints, the Brush and Eraser cursors (REQ-0006-16), the Eraser's mode buttons and the fill styles stay as they are. The panel's drawings are not picture pixels; each Brush and Eraser footprint shown equals REQ-0006-05's footprint (AS-8).

## Edge cases
- A larger text size: the default width and the limit grow with the text scale factor (REQ-0008-07, REQ-0030-09); the footprints stay one logical pixel per picture pixel.
- A fractional desktop scale (125 %, 150 %): the footprints are drawn at the next integer scale and shown by the desktop; looked at in the fractional-scale check of `docs/phase2-plan.md`, not a test here.
- A window too short for the toolbox and the panel: the column scrolls, as REQ-0006's edge case says.
- A drag on the edge while a stroke or a shape is held on the canvas: not possible, the button is already down.

## Out of scope
- Changing the presets themselves (more sizes, a free size), and the Magnifier, Select and fill-style options.
- Moving the edge from the keyboard, an accepted exception to REQ-0022's keyboard paths (settled 2026-11-07 under JOD's instruction to settle it): the width is a view comfort, the default width shows every preset, every preset stays reachable and named for the keyboard and a screen reader as before (REQ-0022-02), and a focusable edge would add a stop to the Tab order of 0022's walk for no task a keyboard user needs.
- More tool columns in a wider toolbox.

## Open questions

## Plan
**Approach (as built after the G2 change, 2026-11-08).** All in `src/ui/tool-options.c`, plus a handle in the window, one key in the settings and the look in `style.css`. Each group of options is a well (`.options-well`: a grid or box in the view's color with a border); each option is a cell (`button.cell`: no outline, the chosen one tinted with the accent mixed into the window's color and outlined in the accent, as the chosen tool button, `.tool-grid button:checked`). A size cell holds only a drawing area: the footprint in the text color made opaque, whole logical pixels, no smoothing. The toolbox width is a `width-request` on `toolbox_panel` set by the window; a 6 px handle at the canvas area's left edge has a drag gesture, a double-click gesture and the `ew-resize` cursor; the options panel stays centered at its size. The status bar has one field, `EASEL_STATUS_PRESET`, as wide as the active tool's longest name.

**Files.** `src/ui/tool-options.{c,h}` (layouts, swatch drawing, number labels, `set_width`, `get_preset_name`); `src/ui/window.c` and `window.ui` (the handle, the width, the half-window clamp, start-up width, the write); `src/ui/status-bar.{c,h}` (the field); `src/core/settings.{c,h}` (`toolbox_width`, its parse and clamp); `src/ui/style.css` (the handle's look, if any); tests in `tests/core/test-settings.c` and `tests/ui/test-window.c`.

**Interfaces.**
- core: `EaselSettings.toolbox_width` (0 = the default); parse: a whole number, else 0; clamped to [0, 240]; a value under the default's is taken as the default by the window, which alone knows the default.
- core: `int easel_settings_toolbox_width (const char *text, size_t len)`, `EASEL_TOOLBOX_WIDTH_MAX` 240.
- ui, as built: `GStrv easel_tool_options_size_names (EaselTool)` and `int easel_tool_options_size_index (EaselToolOptions *, EaselTool, int line_width)` (the words and the selected one, for the status bar); `void easel_status_bar_set_preset (EaselStatusBar *, const char *text, const char *const *names)` and the field `EASEL_STATUS_PRESET`; `int easel_window_get_toolbox_width (EaselWindow *)`, `void easel_window_drag_toolbox_edge (EaselWindow *, double dx, gboolean end)`, `easel_window_cancel_toolbox_drag`, `easel_window_reset_toolbox_width` (what the edge's gestures call, so tests drive them), `GtkWidget *easel_window_get_toolbox_edge (EaselWindow *)`.
- As built: the edge is a 6 px overlay at the canvas area's left edge, next to the toolbox's 1 px line, so the toolbox keeps its 87 px; the options panel sits in a non-scrolling `GtkScrolledWindow` so its names never ask for more width than the toolbox has; the toolbox panel's `hexpand` is set FALSE, since the named form's expanding buttons would otherwise make the toolbox share the canvas area's space.
- The text scale factor: `gtk-xft-dpi` / (96 × 1024), read where REQ-0008-07's text-size cases read it.

**Readers of what changes.** The arrow-key focus moves among the option buttons by grid rows and columns (REQ-0022-02): the re-attached grid keeps that true, checked by 0022's cases. The toolbox width: `as6_toolbox_width_is_the_same_for_every_tool` (0008) and every case that measures the toolbox or the canvas position (the canvas starts after it: the ui cases compute canvas points from the laid-out canvas, so they follow); the brush grid's positions: `as1_toolbox_and_options` (0006) and the tests that click a preset by index (by index, not by place: unchanged); the status bar's fields: 0004's AS-8 and 0021's Frame time cases.

**Risks.**
- R1 The Eraser's "10" beside a 14 px swatch may not fit the column at the default width (about 38 px per button). Probed in T001 by measuring the shown buttons; fallback: the swatch and number tighter, never a wider default toolbox (REQ-0008-07).
- R2 A `width-request` on the toolbox panel next to a `hhomogeneous` stack: the panel must not grow past the width set when the named form's labels are long. Probed in T001; fallback: the named labels ellipsize, and the form switches only when they fit, so they never need to.
- R3 The text scale factor from `gtk-xft-dpi`: probed in T001 at 100 % and 150 % text against REQ-0008-07's measured widths.
- R5 (found in T007; JOD's yes 2026-11-08, REQ-0030-13 and AS-7 reworded) The toolbox's width is a size request, so the window cannot be made narrower than the kept toolbox and the canvas area's least width: with the toolbox at 240 px the window stopped at 601 px when `as7` asked for 400. Half of 601 is more than 240, so at the default text size REQ-0030-13's narrower toolbox shows only where the window is given less than it asks for (a small screen, a larger text size). The other way, a toolbox that asks only for its default width and takes the rest as natural width, was dropped: GTK shares spare width between the toolbox and the canvas, whose natural width is the picture's, so the toolbox would not get its kept width even in a wide window. Proposed wording for REQ-0030-13: "The window shall not be made narrower than the toolbox's width and the canvas area's least width; where the window has less than twice the kept width (a small screen, a large text size), the toolbox shows narrower ...". `as7` asserts it.
- R4 (settled, JOD 2026-11-07) A failed write of the width is silent with a logged warning and a retry at the next drag's end (REQ-0030-12), not Preferences' dialog. Tested in T005 with a read-only file, then made writable.

# 0012 Gradient

Status: implemented
Depends on: 0001, 0002, 0003, 0004, 0006, 0007, 0010, 0011

## Why
The Gradient tool is one of the additions to XP Paint's toolbox (`docs/reference/new-behaviors.md`): a drag sets a direction and a
length, the drawing color fades along it to transparent, and a second gradient lays over the first instead of replacing it.
Like the curve and the polygon it waits after the drag, until a click, a new drag or Enter applies it or Esc cancels it; it is the third "pending" thing of the app (`docs/design.md`, Pending state), and it follows the rules of 0011 for
what puts it down and what drops it.
Decisions by JOD on 2026-10-19: the pixels before the start get the full color and those beyond the end are left alone; the
button goes after Fill and has no options. Decisions on 2026-10-20 after trying it: the colors of a waiting gradient follow the
palette; a click, or a new drag, applies a waiting gradient; its two ends have markers that can be dragged.

## Requirements
Throughout, colors are written 0xAARRGGBB with straight (not premultiplied) channels (ADR-0002), and round (x / y), for whole numbers x >= 0 and y > 0, is the whole number (2x + y) div 2y: to nearest, a half rounded up.

### The tool
- REQ-0012-01 (Pointer note, Gradient types, 2026-11-08: the options panel shows the three types for Gradient, REQ-0032-01; without this note the text below holds.) The toolbox shall show Gradient, with the tooltip "Gradient", after Fill in the final order of REQ-0006-01, so that the toolbox is, in two columns, row by row: Pan, Magnifier / Eraser, Pick Color / Fill, Gradient / Pencil, Brush / Airbrush, Line / Curve, Rectangle / Rounded Rectangle, Ellipse / Polygon. Clicking it makes it the active tool (REQ-0001-09). The options panel shows nothing for it, as for Pan and Pencil (REQ-0008-07: the toolbox keeps one width). Numpad + and Numpad − do nothing with Gradient (as REQ-0007-14 for Fill, Pick Color and the Magnifier); Ctrl+Numpad + and − keep zooming (REQ-0004-04). (Selection: Select comes after the Magnifier and every later button moves one place on, REQ-0013-01.)
- REQ-0012-02 The status bar hint (REQ-0004-09) shall be "Fades the drawing color to transparent along a drag. A click or Enter applies it, Esc cancels it." While Gradient is active the cursor over the canvas area shall be the crosshair of REQ-0010-15 (the resize cursor wins over a handle, REQ-0004-17).

### The gradient
- REQ-0012-03 (Pointer note, Gradient types, 2026-11-08: the gradient shown is the chosen type's, REQ-0032-04 to -07; Linear is this spec's; without this note the text below holds.) When the user presses the primary or the secondary button in the canvas area (on a resize handle: REQ-0012-12) with Gradient and drags, the app shall show a gradient from the pixel under the press (the start) to the pixel under the pointer (the end), following the pointer, without changing the picture. A press may start outside the picture but inside the canvas area; while the button is held the pointer may go outside the picture, the canvas area or the window (REQ-0010-21); only the pixels inside the picture show and are applied, and any pointer position gives the result of REQ-0012-04. Shift constrains the end as it constrains a Line (REQ-0010-14), and pressing or releasing Shift while the button is held changes the end at once, without a pointer move. The release leaves the gradient waiting (REQ-0012-07). The middle button does nothing.
- REQ-0012-04 (Pointer note, Gradient types, 2026-11-08: the opacity below is Linear's; Radial and Reflected give their own, REQ-0032-05, -06; the color rule holds for every type.) The drawing color shall be the foreground color for a gradient the primary button started and the background color for one the secondary button started, and it shall follow that color while the gradient waits or is being dragged: a change of the foreground or the background color (a palette click, Ctrl+Shift+X, the color chooser) recolors the gradient at once, the preview and the applied pixels being the same. A new gradient takes the color current at its press. At a picture pixel p, with p, the start S and the end E taken at pixel centers, L the square of the distance from S to E in pixels, and d the dot product of p - S and E - S limited to the range 0 to L, the gradient's opacity at p is round (A (L - d) / L), A being the drawing color's own opacity (its alpha, 0 to 255). So it is the color's own opacity at the start and everywhere behind it, it falls in a straight line to 0 at the end, and it is 0, so the pixel is left alone, at the end and beyond; the lines of equal opacity are perpendicular to the drag.
- REQ-0012-05 Applying shall lay the gradient over the picture, pixel by pixel, with no antialiasing (P4): for a picture pixel with the color channels dr, dg, db and the alpha da, and the gradient's color channels sr, sg, sb with the opacity a of REQ-0012-04, a pixel with a = 0 is left as it is; any other pixel gets the alpha round (M / 255) and each channel round (N / M), where M = 255 a + da (255 - a) and, for the red channel, N = sr a 255 + dr da (255 - a) (green and blue alike). So over an opaque pixel a channel becomes round ((s a + d (255 - a)) / 255), over a fully transparent pixel the color is the gradient's with its opacity a, and a second gradient applied later lays over the first, whose pixels are then the picture.
- REQ-0012-06 A drag that ends, after Shift's constraint, on the pixel where it started (a click) shall make no new gradient: with none waiting, nothing is pending and nothing is shown; with one waiting, the click applies it as REQ-0012-08 says.

### Waiting, applying, moving the ends
- REQ-0012-07 A gradient that waits shall be pending as a curve or polygon is, and the rules of 0011 apply to it as they are written there, with Gradient in place of the curve and polygon: it is shown over the picture as it will be applied (except as REQ-0012-09 says), the pixels shown being exactly the pixels applied, with the picture unchanged, no time limit and no timer (REQ-0011-09); Esc and Undo drop it, Undo being available for it and Redo not, a press of either button in the canvas area applies it (REQ-0012-08), a middle-button press and a right-click on the palette keeping it (REQ-0011-11); another tool, Save, Save As, New, Open, Quit and an Image action apply it as shown, then carry out the action (REQ-0011-12); View actions, scrolling, zooming and a lost focus leave it waiting (REQ-0011-12); while the button of a drag is held the rules of REQ-0011-13 apply: a lost release or a lost focus ends the drag as last shown and leaves the gradient waiting, the press that shows a lost release then acting as a press of its own (REQ-0012-08), and Save or Quit applies it. (Pointer note, Clipboard, 2026-10-22: Paste and Paste From… apply or drop a pending gradient as a tool change does, but not when the oversize prompt or the file dialog is cancelled, REQ-0016-05, -07, -08.)
- REQ-0012-08 While a gradient waits, a press of either button in the canvas area, not on a marker (REQ-0012-13) or a resize handle (REQ-0012-12), shall apply the waiting gradient as shown (REQ-0012-09) and start a new drag with that button, its start at that press. A release on the new start pixel makes no new gradient (REQ-0012-06), so the press has only applied the waiting one. If applying fails (REQ-0012-09) the dialog shows, the gradient is dropped and the press starts no drag. While the new drag is held, Esc, the other button's press and Undo drop the new drag only; the applied gradient stays applied.
- REQ-0012-09 (Pointer note, Print, 2026-11-03: after a failed put-down Print… does not go on, REQ-0026-12.) Applying a gradient shall be one undo step (REQ-0002-01) and mark the picture modified; one that changes no pixel (no pixel's value would change: an opacity of 0, or a drawing color the picture already has where the gradient is) adds no step and no mark (REQ-0010-07). The step costs at most what REQ-0002-16 allows for the pixels it changes. If the memory to show the gradient or to apply it cannot be had, then the app shall show an error dialog with the heading "Could Not Apply the Gradient" and the body "There is not enough memory to apply this gradient.", when the gradient is applied (when the drag is released, if the gradient cannot be shown), drop the gradient and change nothing (as REQ-0010-20 and REQ-0011-15); the action that applied it (Save, Save As, New, Open, Quit, another tool, an Image action or a press on a resize handle) then goes on, without the gradient; a step larger than the whole budget is kept as the only step (REQ-0002-17). A gradient that already waits and cannot be shown at the current view (for lack of memory, for example at the fit zoom of a very large picture) stays waiting, unshown, with the picture as it is (its markers and guide line still shown, REQ-0012-13) and no dialog; it is shown again when the view allows it, and Enter still applies it as it is, which may then fail as above.
- REQ-0012-10 A first press with Gradient shall be ignored when a press of a shape tool is (REQ-0010-17: before the picture exists, with a dialog, the quit prompt or the color chooser open, or during a resize drag).
- REQ-0012-11 The Enter key (the main keyboard's and the keypad's) shall apply a waiting gradient when no button is held, and do nothing to it while one is held; with no gradient waiting the app shall not take Enter (a focused button gets it as usual); with a dialog, the quit prompt or the color chooser open, Enter and Esc act on that and not on the gradient (REQ-0011-12).
- REQ-0012-12 A press on a resize handle shall follow the handle's rules only, not the drop rule of REQ-0011-11: a primary press applies a waiting gradient as shown and starts the resize, whichever button started the gradient; a secondary or a middle press does nothing and keeps it waiting (REQ-0004-16, REQ-0011-12).
- REQ-0012-13 A waiting gradient shall show a small square marker on its start and on its end, over the picture and not part of the applied pixels. A press of either button on a marker (hit within a few screen pixels, the number set in the Plan) shall move that end: while the button is held the end follows the pointer, Shift constrains it as for a drag (REQ-0010-14), the release leaves the gradient waiting, a lost release or a lost focus end the move as last shown (REQ-0011-13), Esc, the other button's press and Undo cancel the move (the end goes back where it was before the press and the gradient keeps waiting, where Esc with no move in progress drops it, REQ-0012-07), and an end moved onto the other end's pixel leaves the gradient as it was before the press. A press on a marker takes precedence over REQ-0012-08 but not over a resize handle (REQ-0012-12). Settled on 2026-10-20: the markers look like the resize handles of REQ-0004-16 in size (5 x 5 logical pixels, centered on the end's pixel) but are white with a 1-pixel black border so that they show on any color; they are shown while the gradient waits and while one of its ends is moved, not during a drag that makes a new gradient; over a marker the cursor is the move cursor. When a press is within the hit areas of both markers (a short gradient), it takes the marker whose center is nearest the pointer, the end's on a tie. Between the two markers a guide line shall be shown, from the start marker's center to the end marker's, 1 logical pixel wide, in dashes of 4 logical pixels alternating black and white, under the markers, over the picture and not part of the applied pixels; it is shown exactly when the markers are. (Pointer note, Tool cursors, 2026-11-06: the cursors named here are drawn by Easel in black with a white outline; which cursor shows where stays as here.)

## Edge cases
- A drag of 1 pixel to a horizontal or vertical neighbour: the start's pixel and all behind it get the full color, the end's pixel and all beyond are left alone, no pixel between. To a diagonal neighbour: the two pixels beside the diagonal get half the opacity (AS-18).
- A drag wholly outside the picture: only the part of the ramp over the picture shows; with the whole picture behind the start, the whole picture takes the full color; with the whole picture beyond the end, nothing changes and no step is added.
- A foreground of alpha 0, or a color equal to the picture over every pixel it covers: no pixel changes (REQ-0012-09).
- A picture with transparent pixels: REQ-0012-05; the checkerboard shows through the preview as it does through the picture (REQ-0001-11).
- The largest picture, 10000 x 10000 (REQ-0005-17), under a gradient that covers it: about 100 million pixels changed, a step of over a gigabyte, kept as the only step or refused with the dialog (REQ-0012-09).
- The colors swapped (Ctrl+Shift+X) while a gradient waits: it takes the new colors (REQ-0012-04).
- The same action again while its dialog is open: as 0011.

## Out of scope
- A gradient from one color to another, more than one stop, opacity or length options, dithering.
- A gradient that applies inside a selection only (the selection feature will say so; until then the gradient covers the whole picture) and on a floating selection.
- Keyboard-only drawing (Accessibility).
- Gradient modes (more than the one fade) are a later spec, Gradient modes; Radial and Reflected are Gradient types (0032).
- A fast preview of a very large picture at a zoom below 100 % (a gradient dragged at the fit zoom of an 8000 × 8000 picture takes about 390 ms a frame and holds the picture’s size in memory; see the Plan’s Measurements); it waits for a change to how the picture is drawn below 100 %.

## Plan
### Approach
The gradient's arithmetic is a pure core module, `gradient`: the opacity at a pixel (REQ-0012-04) and the blend of a pixel
(REQ-0012-05), both in exact integers, and the apply, which walks the picture's pixels, blends each one, logs every pixel
that changes and pushes the log as one history step. The log of changed pixels is the one the figures already use: it
leaves `figure.c` for a small shared module, `change-log`, so that the rule learned in 0010 (a log that grows by doubling is
shrunk to its length before it is pushed) lives in one place for both. In the canvas the gradient is a third kind of
pending shape beside the curve and the polygon: the 0011 machinery (pending state, a held stage counted as a stroke, put
down, drop, `pending-changed`, Enter, Esc, Undo, the handle press) is reused as it is; what is new is one stage that is
aimed again by a same-button press, the old line kept aside to come back if the new drag ends on its start, and a preview
that shows the blended pixels instead of runs of one color. The preview is computed in the snapshot from the same core
functions, for the picture pixels the view shows, and drawn as textures placed and filtered as the picture's tiles are, so
that the window shows the same pixels before and after Enter. P5: the plain per-pixel formula comes first; a task measures
a drag and an apply against the frame budget before anything faster is written, and the fallbacks are named in the risks.

### Key interfaces
- `EaselGradient { int x0, y0, x1, y1; uint32_t color; }`, the start and the end in picture pixels, the drawing color.
- `unsigned easel_gradient_opacity (const EaselGradient *g, int x, int y)`: REQ-0012-04, 0 to 255.
- `uint32_t easel_gradient_blend (uint32_t picture, uint32_t color, unsigned a)`: REQ-0012-05.

### What grows
Per apply: the log, 12 bytes per changed pixel plus 64 for the step, shrunk to its length before the push (so what the
history holds is what it counts: a test compares the heap held with `easel_history_bytes`, as 0010 T012 does). A gradient
over a whole 10000 x 10000 picture changes up to 100 million pixels, a log of 1.2 GB, kept as the only step or refused.
Per frame of the preview: one texture for each tile in view, the tile's picture pixels laid over, 4 bytes a pixel, held
by the frame's render node until the next frame (about 20 MB at 100 % on a 1920 x 1200 view, the whole picture at the fit
zoom of a large one: see Measurements). Per pending gradient: two points and the line kept aside while it is aimed again.

whole 8000 x 8000 picture (time and peak memory). The numbers go here; anything over budget goes to JOD before a fix.

### Constitution check

### Risks
- **The preview's cost:** blending every visible picture pixel each frame may be over a frame at 1920 x 1080 and more so at the
  fit zoom of 8000 x 8000 (64 million pixels in view). Checked by the measurement task T008, in the group of the preview; fallbacks,
  only on JOD's word: blend only the picture pixels the view samples at a zoom below 100 % (one per device pixel), compute the
  opacity of a row incrementally instead of with a division per pixel, and a fast path for opaque picture pixels.
- **The preview equals the drawing below 100 %:** the preview's textures must be placed and filtered as the picture's tiles
  are, so that the same picture pixel lands on each device pixel; checked by rendering the window while the gradient waits

  preview is relied on.

### Change of 2026-10-20 (recolor, a click or a press applies, markers)
Approach. The core is unchanged: the opacity, the blend, `easel_gradient_lay` and the apply stay as they are. All of the change
is in the canvas, the window's hint, and the tests.
- **The color follows the palette (REQ-0012-04).** The canvas keeps the button that started the gradient (`grad_button`) instead
  of a color: `shown_gradient` takes `foreground` or `background` at that moment, so the preview and the apply always use the
  current color. `easel_canvas_set_colors`, the one function every color change reaches (the window's `set_colors`: palette,
  Ctrl+Shift+X, chooser), queues a redraw when a gradient is pending.
- **A click or a press applies (REQ-0012-06, -07, -08).** In `easel_canvas_press_timed`, a press of either button while a
  gradient waits (after the handle rule of REQ-0012-12 and the marker check) calls `put_down_pending`, which now returns its
  result; on DONE or NOTHING the press goes on as a first press of Gradient (the same code), on NO_MEMORY it stops (the dialog
  comes through `gradient-failed`). A release on the start pixel already leaves nothing pending (REQ-0012-06). While the new
  drag is held, Esc, Undo and the other button reach `easel_canvas_drop`, which drops only it: the applied gradient is already
  a step of the history. The aiming machinery of T009 (`aim_old`, `aim_has_old`, the kept line in `shown_gradient`) is removed.
- **Markers (REQ-0012-13).** `append_gradient_markers`, after the tiles in the snapshot: two 5 x 5 logical squares, white with
  a 1-pixel black border, centered on the start and the end pixel at the current zoom, shown while the gradient waits and while
  an end is moved. `marker_at (self, x, y)` gives the start, the end or none, with a hit area of 9 x 9 logical pixels around
  each center; when both are hit, the one whose center is nearest the pointer, the end's on a tie (AS-23); the handle test comes first. A press on a marker sets `pend_held`
  and `moving` (start or end) and keeps the line as it was (`move_old`); `pend_follow` moves that end, Shift constraining it
  from the other end as for Line. The move ends in `end_pending_stage` (release, lost release, lost focus): an end on the other
  end's pixel puts `move_old` back. `easel_canvas_drop` is the one place Esc, Undo and the other button's press reach: while an
  end is moved it cancels the move (`move_old` back, the gradient still waiting) instead of dropping. The hover of a marker sets
  the move cursor in `update_cursor`, after the handle's resize cursor (the handle wins).
- **Hint (REQ-0012-02):** the new text in `tool_hint`.
- **The guide line (REQ-0012-13, added after M3, T018).** `append_gradient_markers` first strokes the line between the
  markers' centers (`GskPath`, 1 logical pixel: white, then black dashes of 4), then draws the markers over it; it uses the
  markers' rectangles, so it is shown exactly when they are. The test hook `easel_canvas_set_guides_hidden` (and its getter)
  leaves the markers and the line out of the rendering while they still take presses: `gradient_with_black` hides them so
  that a rendered region is the picture alone, and AS-16, AS-21 and AS-25 show them; AS-16 compares the waiting window with
  the guides hidden against the applied one exactly, and as shown only near the markers and the line.
- **An unshown gradient (REQ-0012-09):** the markers and the line do not depend on the preview's memory, so they stay shown
  while the blended pixels cannot be; `as14_waiting_gradient_unshown` checks the markers then.
